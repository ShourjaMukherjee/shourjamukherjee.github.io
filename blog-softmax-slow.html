A confidently wrong policy barely learns

I used to assume that the more wrong a policy is, the bigger the correction it gets. With a softmax policy trained by policy gradient it goes the other way, and the reason is mechanical. The policy only learns from the actions it samples. If it puts 99.9% on the wrong action, it tries the right one about once in a thousand steps, so it gets about one useful update per thousand.

Mei et al. make this precise. Softmax policy gradient does converge, at a rate of 1/t, and the bound carries a constant of 1/c², where c is the smallest probability the best action has at any point during training. If the best action starts at 0.1%, that constant is at least a million. They also show that you can't get rid of that dependence.

Here is a toy version. There are two buttons, B pays out 70% of the time and A pays 30%. I start the policy leaning toward A by some amount and count the steps until it picks B 90% of the time.

import math, random, statistics

def steps_to_learn(p_wrong, seed):
    random.seed(seed)
    pay = [0.3, 0.7]
    pref = [math.log(p_wrong / (1 - p_wrong)), 0.0]
    for t in range(1_000_000):
        p0 = 1 / (1 + math.exp(pref[1] - pref[0]))
        p = [p0, 1 - p0]
        if p[1] > 0.9:
            return t
        a = 0 if random.random() < p0 else 1
        if random.random() < pay[a]:
            for i in range(2):
                pref[i] += 0.1 * ((1 - p[i]) if i == a else -p[i])

for p_wrong in (0.5, 0.9, 0.99, 0.999):
    runs = [steps_to_learn(p_wrong, s) for s in range(100)]
    print(p_wrong, statistics.median(runs))

Those numbers come from my own run with sampled rewards, whereas the paper analyses the exact gradient, but the pattern is the same. Each extra nine of misplaced confidence costs a lot more than the one before it.

The usual remedy is an entropy bonus, which rewards the policy a little for staying uncertain so that it keeps sampling every action occasionally. The paper shows that with entropy regularisation the convergence rate improves from 1/t to exponential.

Paper: Mei, Xiao, Szepesvári, Schuurmans. On the Global Convergence Rates of Softmax Policy Gradient Methods (ICML 2020)
