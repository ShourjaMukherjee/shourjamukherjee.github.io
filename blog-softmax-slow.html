Policy gradients are slowest when most wrong

November 2026

You would expect a big error to give a big correction. With a softmax policy it is the opposite.

A policy gradient learns from what it samples. If the policy is 99.9% sure of the wrong action, it tries the right one once in a thousand steps. That is one useful lesson per thousand.

What the paper proves

Softmax policy gradient does converge, at rate 1/t. But the bound has a constant in front: 1/c², where c is the lowest probability the best action ever has during training.

Start with the best action at 0.1% and that constant is at least a million. The paper is direct about it: for a bad start c can be tiny, and you cannot remove the dependence.

Try it

Two buttons. B pays 70% of the time, A pays 30%. Start the policy leaning toward A and count the steps until it picks B 90% of the time.

These numbers are from my own toy run with sampled rewards, not from the paper. The paper's setting uses the exact gradient. The shape is the same: each extra 9 costs far more than the last.

import math, random, statistics

def steps_to_learn(p_wrong, seed):
    random.seed(seed)
    pay = [0.3, 0.7]
    pref = [math.log(p_wrong / (1 - p_wrong)), 0.0]
    for t in range(1_000_000):
        p0 = 1 / (1 + math.exp(pref[1] - pref[0]))
        p = [p0, 1 - p0]
        if p[1] > 0.9:
            return t
        a = 0 if random.random() < p0 else 1
        if random.random() < pay[a]:
            for i in range(2):
                pref[i] += 0.1 * ((1 - p[i]) if i == a else -p[i])

for p_wrong in (0.5, 0.9, 0.99, 0.999):
    runs = [steps_to_learn(p_wrong, s) for s in range(100)]
    print(p_wrong, statistics.median(runs))

The fix

Entropy regularization: pay the policy a little to stay unsure. The paper shows the rate goes from 1/t to exponential. The policy never gets sure enough to stop looking.

Paper: Mei, Xiao, Szepesvári, Schuurmans. On the Global Convergence Rates of Softmax Policy Gradient Methods (ICML 2020)
