Why RL forgets less

November 2026

Teach a model a new task with RL and it keeps its old skills. Teach it the same task with SFT and it loses them.

That is the graph. I never got how they showed why. It is four steps.

1. Find what predicts forgetting

They built a toy task: say if an MNIST digit is even or odd. For an even image, any even label counts. So there are many correct ways to answer.

Then they fine-tuned many models, with RL and with SFT, and plotted forgetting against one number: how far the model's outputs moved from the base model, on the new task, in KL divergence.

RL points and SFT points fell on one curve. R² = 0.96 on the toy task, 0.71 on real LLMs. Weight change, the usual suspect, got 0.34 to 0.58.

2. Break the tie with a trick

If distance is the cause, SFT with the right labels must also forget less. In the toy task you can compute the labels that stay closest to the base model. They trained SFT on those.

It forgot less than RL. So RL has no magic. It only moves less.

3. Ask why RL moves less

RL differs from SFT in two ways: it learns from its own samples, and it sees wrong answers. Which one matters? They tested all four mixes.

Correct onlyCorrect + wrong
Own samples1-0 REINFORCE: keeps old skillsGRPO: keeps old skills
Outside dataSFT: forgetsSimPO: forgets

The row decides, not the column. It is the model's own samples.

4. The proof, in one example

Ask the base model "2 + 2?".

"4""four""5"KL from base
Base model50%30%20%0
RL: keep own correct samples62.5%37.5%0%0.22
SFT: labels all say "four"0%100%0%1.20

RL deletes the wrong answer and keeps the 5:3 ratio the model already had. Their Lemma 5.1 says this is the closest fully correct distribution to the base. No other one is nearer.

SFT copies whatever the labels say. The labels do not know what the model liked before.

Theorem 5.2 repeats that step until convergence: with a 0/1 reward, policy gradient ends at the correct policy closest to where it started.

The catch

The theorem needs a simple setting: binary reward, a convex family of policies. And nobody knows yet why moving far on the new task damages the old ones. The law is measured, not explained.

Paper: Shenfeld, Pari, Agrawal. RL's Razor: Why Online Reinforcement Learning Forgets Less (2025)
