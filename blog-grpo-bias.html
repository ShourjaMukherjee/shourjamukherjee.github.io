GRPO has two thumbs on the scale

November 2026

GRPO divides by two things. Both divisions look harmless. Both are biases.

Division 1: the group's spread

GRPO scores each answer against the group: reward, minus the group mean, divided by the group standard deviation.

rewards [0,    1,    0,    0   ]  →  [-0.58, 1.73, -0.58, -0.58]
rewards [0.50, 0.51, 0.50, 0.50]  →  [-0.58, 1.73, -0.58, -0.58]

Same output. A clear win and a rounding error get the same push.

The paper states the effect at the question level: questions where the rewards barely differ, the too-easy and the too-hard, get more weight, not less.

Division 2: the answer's length

GRPO also averages the loss over the tokens of each answer. So a long answer gets a smaller push per token.

AnswerShortLong
CorrectLarge rewardSmall reward
WrongLarge penaltySmall penalty

Look at the bottom row. The cheapest way to be wrong is to be wrong at length. The paper links this to a known pattern: answers get longer during training, and wrong answers most of all.

The fix

Delete both divisions. That is the full change, and they call it Dr. GRPO. With it, a 7B model reached 43.3% on AIME 2024.

What is left is the plain policy gradient with the group mean as the baseline. The two extra terms were not in the theory to begin with.

Paper: Liu et al. Understanding R1-Zero-Like Training: A Critical Perspective (2025)
