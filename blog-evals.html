Evals, from scratch

November 13, 2026 · 3 min read

An eval is an exam for a model.

You ask questions. The model answers. You grade the answers. The grade tells you how good the model is.

The three parts

  • Dataset. The questions, each with a correct answer.
  • Model. The thing you test.
  • Grader. The rule that says if an answer is right.

A small example

The task: name the capital of a country.

QuestionCorrectModel saidGrade
FranceParisParis1
JapanTokyoTokyo1
AustraliaCanberraSydney0
KenyaNairobiNairobi1
CanadaOttawaOttawa1

Score: 4 of 5, or 80%. That is a full eval.

Four types of grader

GraderGood forWeak point
Exact matchOne correct answerFails on "paris" vs "Paris, France"
Code checkMath, code, structured outputYou must write the check
HumanOpen answers, such as essaysSlow and costly
Model as judgeOpen answers, at scaleThe judge also makes errors

Use the simplest grader that works.

Five rules

  1. Do not train on the exam. A student who saw the paper before the exam gets 100%. That score means nothing.
  2. Keep the exam fixed. If the questions change, you cannot compare two scores.
  3. Look at the errors. Read the wrong answers. The number does not tell you why.
  4. Split the score. 80% overall can be 99% on easy questions and 20% on hard questions.
  5. Write down what you ran. Model version, dataset version, grader version. Without these, nobody can repeat the result.

Small exams are noisy

Flip a fair coin 10 times. You can get 7 heads. The coin is still fair.

Evals have the same problem. With 100 questions, a score of 80% has an error of about ±8 points. So 80% and 83% are possibly the same model quality.

More questions give a smaller error. With 1,000 questions, the error is about ±2.5 points.

Fixed questions, a clear grader, and a record of what you ran. Everything else is detail.
