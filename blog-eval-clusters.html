Your eval has fewer questions than you think

November 2026

An eval with 10,000 questions about 500 passages can behave more like an eval with 500 questions.

The usual error bar treats each question as an independent coin flip. More questions, smaller bar.

But many evals ask several questions about one passage. If the model misreads the passage, it misses all of them together. Those questions are one flip, counted many times.

How much it matters

The paper computes the error bar both ways on real evals.

EvalClustered ÷ naive
DROP3.05×
MGSM1.88×
RACE-H1.10×

On DROP the honest standard error is 1.34, not 0.44. A gap between two models that looked solid can be inside the noise.

The reverse trick

Correlation also helps you, if you use it. Two models tend to fail the same hard questions. So compare them question by question and not score against score.

The variance of the difference drops by twice the covariance between the two models' scores. Same data, tighter answer, no new questions.

What to do

  • Questions share a passage, a document, or a template: use clustered standard errors.
  • Two models, same questions: use paired differences.
  • The paper's worked example needs about 1,000 questions to detect a 3-point gap.

Paper: Miller. Adding Error Bars to Evals: A Statistical Approach to Language Model Evaluations (2024)
